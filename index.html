Robots can already cross rough ground in controlled conditions. What we can’t yet do is send one somewhere it has never seen, through dust, dark or loose debris, and rely on it to find its own way. Navigation is more than pathfinding: a route on a map only matters if the body can take it, so perception, decisions and movement have to be treated as one problem.

We believe the body and its control have to be developed together. Learning matters because we don’t want to write a new behaviour every time the terrain changes. Repairable, modular hardware matters because we want to keep rebuilding and improving the machine instead of treating it as finished

We want to explore this by building man-portable systems for deploying robots in unstructured terrain, to truly test the limits of how much a machine can work out for itself. Rubble, loose debris and tight gaps are where fixed behaviours break first, and where nobody can tell the robot what’s ahead. We want machines one person can carry in, set down and trust to find their own way through.

We’re early, and we want to test these ideas by building machines and putting them on ground they haven’t seen. If you’re working on these problems, or have somewhere a robot like this could be useful, write to us at hello@modubotics.in.
